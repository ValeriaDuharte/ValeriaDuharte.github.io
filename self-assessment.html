PROFESSIONAL SELF-ASSESSMENT

My Computer Science Journey

Reflecting on the knowledge, skills, and professional development I gained throughout the Computer Science program.

Professional Growth and Preparation

Completing the Computer Science program has helped me develop from someone who could complete individual programming assignments into a more well-rounded computer science professional who can analyze, build, test, improve, and explain software. Throughout the program, I have worked with application development, object-oriented design, software development practices, databases, data structures and algorithms, software testing, secure coding, and emerging technologies.

The variety of coursework required me to approach problems from different perspectives instead of relying on one programming language or one development approach. The CS499 ePortfolio has given me an opportunity to bring those experiences together and demonstrate how the skills I developed throughout the program can be applied to real software problems.

One of the most valuable parts of the program has been learning that software development is an iterative process. Projects rarely work perfectly on the first attempt, and completing a working program is not necessarily the same as completing a reliable or maintainable one. My coursework repeatedly required me to troubleshoot errors, evaluate results, revise implementations, and test changes.

This became especially clear while enhancing my Travlr application for this capstone. Because I was returning to an existing project, I had to understand how the frontend, backend, API, and database worked together before I could make meaningful improvements. That experience strengthened my ability to work with an existing codebase and reinforced the importance of maintainability, testing, and careful debugging.

Collaboration and Communication

My coursework has also helped me understand that technical ability is only one part of being an effective computer science professional. Software is developed for users, organizations, and stakeholders, so developers need to communicate technical information clearly and work within the requirements of a project.

Through discussions, assignments, project documentation, code reviews, and feedback throughout the program, I have practiced explaining technical decisions and considering how a solution meets the needs of its intended audience.

The CS499 code review was especially useful in developing this skill. The assignment required me to explain an existing project, identify areas for improvement, and communicate a plan for enhancements to an audience of peers or a manager. Instead of simply showing code, I had to explain how the system worked, why particular areas needed improvement, and what skills the planned changes would demonstrate.

My experience with feedback throughout the program has also reinforced the importance of revision. I have learned to treat feedback as part of the development process rather than as an indication that a project is finished or unfinished. Reviewing requirements, identifying gaps, making changes, and checking the result against the original goal are practices that I expect to continue using in a professional environment.

Data Structures and Algorithms

The program strengthened my ability to approach problems algorithmically by teaching me to consider how information is represented, processed, and used to make decisions. My work with data structures and algorithms helped me understand that the quality of a solution depends not only on whether it produces an answer, but also on how effectively it processes information and how well the chosen approach fits the problem.

The TreasureMaze/Pirate Intelligent Agent project is the strongest example of this development in my ePortfolio. In that project, I applied deep Q-learning to a pathfinding problem. The agent represented its environment as states, selected actions, received rewards, and stored experiences for later training.

I worked with exploration and exploitation, experience replay, a neural network for estimating Q-values, and a target network to improve training stability. The project required me to connect theoretical algorithmic concepts to an actual working system.

An important lesson from TreasureMaze was learning to evaluate an algorithm using meaningful success criteria instead of relying on a single performance measurement. After 300 epochs, the model reached a 1.000 win rate, but the completion check returned False. I therefore could not assume that the model had learned a reliable solution.

After additional training, the 600-epoch model reached a 0.969 win rate and passed the completion check. This experience strengthened my ability to interpret results critically, troubleshoot unexpected behavior, and use experimentation to improve an algorithmic solution.

Software Engineering and Development

My coursework has given me experience with multiple stages of software development, including planning, design, implementation, testing, debugging, and maintenance. Courses such as software development lifecycle, object-oriented analysis and design, software testing and quality assurance, advanced programming, and full-stack development helped me understand that professional software engineering involves more than writing code.

Structure, readability, testing, documentation, maintainability, and the ability to modify an existing system are all important parts of a successful software solution.

My Travlr project demonstrates how these concepts came together. The application uses Angular for the frontend, Node.js and Express for the backend, and MongoDB for data storage.

During the capstone enhancements, I worked through issues involving the Angular application, API communication, server behavior, and MongoDB connectivity. Troubleshooting these issues required me to isolate problems, examine application behavior, verify connections, and test changes across multiple layers of the system.

This experience improved my confidence in debugging full-stack applications and taught me to consider the system as a connected group of components rather than treating each component independently.

I also developed a better understanding of version control and project organization through my software projects. Working with Git branches and maintaining different versions of projects reinforced the importance of preserving an original implementation while developing enhancements.

Databases and Data Management

Database development has been another important part of my growth. My coursework introduced me to SQL and database concepts and later gave me opportunities to work with database technologies as part of larger applications.

These experiences helped me understand the difference between simply storing information and designing an application that can reliably create, retrieve, update, and manage information.

The database portion of my Travlr enhancement focused on MongoDB and its integration with the rest of the application. I worked on validation, CRUD functionality, error handling, and the retrieval of trip information.

I also had to troubleshoot communication between the database, Express API, and Angular frontend. This showed me how closely database reliability is connected to application reliability.

Working with both relational database concepts and MongoDB has broadened my understanding of how different data technologies can support software applications. It has also helped me recognize the importance of choosing and using data technologies based on the needs of the application.

Security and Responsible Development

Security has been an important part of my computer science education because software must be designed with potential risks in mind. My coursework in secure coding and cybersecurity-related topics introduced me to the importance of protecting data, validating input, considering vulnerabilities, and making security part of the development process rather than something added only after an application is completed.

The capstone enhancement process reinforced these ideas through validation and error handling. While the primary focus of the Travlr database enhancement was reliability and functionality, improving validation also demonstrated why applications should not assume that every request will contain valid or expected information.

Learning to think about unexpected input and failure conditions has changed the way I approach software development. I now consider not only whether an application works under normal conditions, but also how it behaves when something goes wrong.

Professional Goals and Values

The Computer Science program has helped me shape my professional goals around becoming a developer who can understand a problem from both a technical and practical perspective.

I value continuous learning because technology changes quickly, and my coursework demonstrated that the skills needed to solve one problem may be different from those needed to solve another. I want to continue developing my abilities in software development, full-stack technologies, databases, algorithms, and emerging areas of computer science.

I also value persistence and problem solving. Some of the most useful learning experiences in the program came from projects that did not work correctly the first time. Debugging application problems, resolving backend or database connectivity issues, interpreting unexpected machine-learning results, and revising code after testing all required patience and a willingness to investigate instead of giving up.

These experiences have made me more comfortable with ambiguity and with the process of finding a solution when the cause of a problem is not immediately obvious.

As I enter the next stage of my career, I want to apply these skills in an environment where I can continue learning while contributing to meaningful software projects. My goal is not simply to produce code that works, but to contribute to software that is understandable, maintainable, reliable, secure, and useful to its intended users.

How My Artifacts Fit Together

The artifacts in this ePortfolio were selected because they demonstrate different but complementary areas of my development as a computer scientist. The Travlr Getaways application represents my growth in software design and engineering and databases.

It demonstrates my experience working with a full-stack application, analyzing an existing codebase, improving its behavior, troubleshooting communication among application layers, and working with MongoDB.

The TreasureMaze/Pirate Intelligent Agent represents my growth in algorithms and data structures through the application of deep reinforcement learning to a pathfinding problem.

Together, these artifacts demonstrate that my skills extend beyond one type of programming task. Travlr required me to think about application architecture, user-facing functionality, APIs, databases, debugging, and maintainability.

TreasureMaze required me to think about states, actions, rewards, learning strategies, stored experiences, algorithm performance, and evaluation.

The projects therefore provide complementary evidence of my ability to approach both traditional software development and more algorithmically focused problems.

The ePortfolio also demonstrates growth because the artifacts are presented in both their original and enhanced forms. This makes it possible to see not only what I built, but how I evaluated existing work and improved it.

Conclusion

Completing the Computer Science program has given me a stronger technical foundation and a better understanding of what it means to develop software professionally.

I have gained experience with algorithms and data structures, software engineering, databases, testing, security, full-stack development, and emerging technologies. More importantly, I have learned how these areas connect when solving real problems.

The CS499 ePortfolio represents that growth by combining projects that demonstrate different technical strengths and by documenting the reasoning, challenges, and learning behind my enhancements.

I am leaving the program with greater confidence in my ability to learn new technologies, troubleshoot difficult problems, communicate technical ideas, and improve existing software. I recognize that graduation is not the end of that learning process. Computer science is a field that continues to evolve, and I intend to carry the problem-solving, adaptability, and continuous-learning mindset developed throughout this program into my professional career.
